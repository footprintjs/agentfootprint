**The time reader keeps what the person said: "8 AM to 9 AM" is one hour, "yesterday London time" is the London day, and an app can map `PST` to a zone without the library guessing.** Four readings the English reader proposed wrongly are fixed at their owner. A range END said as an o'clock hour now ends AT that hour (`[08:00, 09:00)`), where it used to run to the end of that hour (`08:00–10:00`, shown as "8:00 – 9:59"); a minute end ("to 8:40" → `08:41`), a whole day and a lone "9 AM" still run to the end of their grain, and the confirmation label and the said-parts spelling ask one helper, `widenedGrain`, whether an end was widened. A bare first side takes the second side's meridiem, so "between 8 and 9 PM" is 8 PM – 9 PM, not a 14-hour window (it falls back to AM only when PM would run backwards: "11 to 1 PM"). An ISO time is read as the 24-hour clock it is (`TimeWall.clock: '24h'`), never also as PM. "8 to 9:30" is now ONE unreadable mention instead of a silent read of just "9:30". **A zone the person named is no longer dropped:** a zone written after a day word joins it (`yesterday UTC`, `yesterday Europe/London`), and a place named with `time` (`London time`, `New York time`) is proposed as the ONE zone the runtime's tz database has for it — no word list; `India time`, `Pacific time` or `Server time` name no single zone, so the zone is asked with the person's words. The reading carries a `zone-read` note, stays a proposal the person confirms, and a window they type in the confirmation is recorded in the zone its offsets show, not the app's. **New `.time({ policy: { abbreviations } })`:** the app declares the abbreviations its people write, `{ PST: { zone: 'America/Los_Angeles', offset: '-08:00' } }`; a mapped abbreviation whose zone and letters disagree on the date (`PST` in October) is offered both ways in ONE confirmation (`open: ['abbreviation', 'confirm']`), never corrected. No map ships, and without one every byte is unchanged — an abbreviation is still asked as a zone. The reader's version is now `1.1.0`.
