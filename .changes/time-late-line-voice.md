**The served time line says once that it comes from the library, not the person — and a shifted read is served as what the answer must say.** Under `.time()`, the late time line the model reads (the person's windows and the time limits, appended last to a request) now opens ONCE with one shared note that it is from the library that runs the tools, not from the person and not a correction from them, whichever halves it holds. Before, only the limits half carried an opening, and the windows half read as the person talking: in a paid run, every answer after "the person has not confirmed it" opened "You're right… I apologize" (37 of 37). That sentence is now a conclusion about the results in hand, with no account of the call that ran: "The results for “…” cover the window written into the call — the assistant's own reading of those words. So the answer gives those results and names that window as the assistant's reading of “…”." (A first rewrite that still told the story of the call — "the call that ran carried a window written into the call, not one the person confirmed" — drew the same apology on 7 of 10 answers.) A quote whose time zone the library cannot resolve (an abbreviation outside the app's map) no longer promises a form "which shows its reading": the line says the form asks the person for the zone. A look-back that ran after the clock moved on (a check-in pause) is served to the model as a conclusion: which part of the window its result does not cover, in the person's zone, and that the answer says so and claims nothing about that time. The person's limits block keeps its bytes. Without `.time()` nothing changes; the served-request rebuild (`servedAt`) reproduces the new line byte for byte.
