**The library's careful English reader: the person's time words read without guessing, served to the model in each tool's own form, and asked about only when a tool needs them.** `.time({ reader: englishTimeReader() })` arms a small, dependency-free tokenizer: ISO dates and instants, numeric dates (`10/09/26` — which number is the month stays open for your `dateOrder` policy or the person), clock times (`8 AM`, `8:40`, `20:40`), a range of two (`8 AM to 8:40 AM`, `08:00–08:40`, `between 8 and 9 AM`), a zone written after them (an IANA name, `UTC-07:00`, or an abbreviation such as `PST` kept as written — no abbreviation map ships, so it is asked), today / yesterday / tomorrow, and "last 40 minutes". Every other time phrase it recognises — "yesterday morning", "last week", "3 hours ago", "Friday", "Oct 9", "since 8 AM" — is ONE `unreadable` mention quoting the whole phrase, never a partial reading. Each window the reader settled is now served on every tool that declares a period: one sentence after the tool's description naming the window in that tool's own form ("“yesterday” → start_time 1791442800000, end_time 1791529200000 — a reading of their words, not their words"). A window it could not settle is asked only when a tool whose own rule asks for its period is about to be called: one field for the batch — the zone first ("Which time zone did you mean by “PST” in …?", `format: 'zone'`), then the readings the tool's declared facts allow as labelled `time-range` choices — and the chosen window is written into the tool's own arguments, filed `answered`, the result's note saying the value came from the window the person chose; when every reading breaks the tool's facts nothing is asked and the call is refused with the reason. A tool that assumes its period keeps its default, and without a reader nothing is served or asked — every request byte is unchanged.
