**The library's careful English reader: the person's time words read without guessing, proposed to the person for one-click confirmation when a tool needs them, and — once confirmed — served to the model in each tool's own form.** `.time({ reader: englishTimeReader() })` arms a small, dependency-free tokenizer: ISO dates and instants, numeric dates (`10/09/26` — which number is the month stays open for your `dateOrder` policy or the person), clock times (`8 AM`, `8:40`, `20:40`), a range of two (`8 AM to 8:40 AM`, `08:00–08:40`, `between 8 and 9 AM`), a zone written after them (an IANA name, `UTC-07:00`, or an abbreviation such as `PST` kept as written — no abbreviation map ships, so it is asked), today / yesterday / tomorrow, and "last 40 minutes". Every other time phrase it recognises — "yesterday morning", "last week", "3 hours ago", "Friday", "Oct 9", "since 8 AM" — is ONE `unreadable` mention quoting the whole phrase, never a partial reading. **A reading only proposes — nothing typed in chat is filed as the person's words.** Every window read from a message is offered through the time ask as a pre-filled, editable one-click confirmation that names the window AND its zone ("I read “yesterday” as Thu, Oct 8, 2026, PDT in America/Los_Angeles — is that right?"), asked only when a tool that declares a period is about to be called with its period left out — the zone first when the person wrote an abbreviation ("Which time zone did you mean by “PST” in …?", `format: 'zone'`), then the readings the tool's declared facts allow as labelled `time-range` choices, free entry open. The person's answer is written into the tool's own arguments, filed `answered`, and recorded as a new `time-answer` row (`how: 'confirmed'` for the click on the offered reading, `'edited'` for a window they wrote); the rest of the turn's calls are filled from it, and each tool that declares a period is then served one sentence naming that window in its own form with its source ("“yesterday” → start_time 1791442800000, end_time 1791529200000 (the window the person confirmed when asked what their words meant)"). This applies to every reader: a `rule` reader's reading is no longer filed as `said`, and a tool whose rule ASSUMES its period is asked too — its default never stands in for words the person wrote. When every reading breaks the tool's facts nothing is asked and the call is refused with the reason. Without a reader nothing is served or asked — every request byte is unchanged.
